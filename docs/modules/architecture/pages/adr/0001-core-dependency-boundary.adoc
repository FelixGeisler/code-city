= ADR 0001: Core/edge source-dependency boundary

Status:: Accepted

== Context

The accepted M1 contracts support a stable boundary that lets deterministic
policy remain independent of change-prone GitHub/repository I/O and renderer
technology. The original proposal went further by defining application and
domain layers, allowing specific dependencies between them, and classifying
framework and storage concerns. Those choices are not needed for the minimum
stable boundary and belong to later decisions.

== Decision

There is one conceptual *core* and one collective *edge/adapters* side. These
are responsibility boundaries, not packages, processes, deployment nodes, or
a count of components.

This ADR governs only source dependencies that cross that conceptual boundary:

* `edge/adapters → core` is allowed: source at the edge may depend on core
  source. This permission does not require every edge source to depend on core.
* `core → edge/adapters` is forbidden.

A source-dependency arrow means that source on the arrow's left may depend on
source on its right. It does not depict or constrain runtime calls, control
flow, or data flow.

GitHub/repository I/O and renderer technology are edge concerns and remain
outside the core. Renderer-neutral canonical policy is not renderer technology
and may be assigned to the core by the later building-block decision.

This ADR establishes no dependency rule inside the core or inside
edge/adapters. It creates no application/domain split, no direct
adapter-to-domain grant, and no framework or storage category, existence
assumption, or placement rule.

== Rationale

This is the smallest stable boundary supported by the accepted M1 contracts.
It keeps core source independent of GitHub/repository I/O and renderer
technology while leaving topology and internal building-block and port choices
to https://github.com/FelixGeisler/code-city/issues/446[#446] and
https://github.com/FelixGeisler/code-city/issues/462[#462]. Narrowing ADR 0001
in place removes accidental commitments from its proposed form without losing
its identity and history.

== Downside

The rule is intentionally less prescriptive. Internal structure, ports,
concrete source/package mapping, and enforcement remain to later accepted
decisions, and adapters may need translation at the boundary.

== Consequences

* Core source must not acquire a source dependency on GitHub/repository I/O,
  renderer technology, or other source assigned to edge/adapters.
* https://github.com/FelixGeisler/code-city/issues/446[#446] consumes this
  conceptual boundary but still owns placement, target, process/system/trust
  boundaries, runtime flow, cleanup ownership, and feasible enforcement
  locations. It must not authorize a core-to-edge source dependency. This ADR
  selects none of those choices.
* https://github.com/FelixGeisler/code-city/issues/462[#462] owns internal core
  and edge responsibilities, any application/domain split, all internal
  dependency directions, conceptual ports and ownership, and whether a
  narrower adapter-to-core rule is needed. It must keep GitHub/repository I/O
  and renderer technology outside the core, may place renderer-neutral
  canonical policy in the core, and must preserve the prohibition on
  core-to-edge source dependencies. It must not treat this ADR as granting a
  direct adapter-to-domain dependency.
* After the accepted M0 mappings exist,
  https://github.com/FelixGeisler/code-city/issues/452[#452] owns the concrete
  source/package mapping and enforcement mechanism. Its evidence must accept
  the governed edge-to-core direction and reject a representative core-to-edge
  source dependency. It must not encode an internal core/edge,
  application/domain, direct adapter-to-domain, framework, or storage rule
  that this ADR does not establish. This ADR chooses no package layout, tool,
  check implementation, or enforcement location.

== Rejected alternatives

* *Accept ADR 0001 unchanged.* Rejected because its application/domain split,
  direct adapter-to-domain permission, and framework/storage classifications
  would decide #462 or assume absent M1 concerns.
* *Create a successor ADR.* Rejected because an in-place narrowing preserves
  the same decision identity without duplicate history.
* *Omit the boundary or allow core-to-edge source dependencies.* Rejected
  because either would permit core policy to couple to the external mechanisms
  this decision must isolate.
